2. Parents and families: supporting a child

A planning page for families adjusting to a child's diagnosis. Work with your child's healthcare team and a celiac-experienced dietitian on individual needs.

Helping siblings understand

Use simple, age-appropriate language. Explain that celiac disease is not contagious, that the child has not done anything wrong, and that adults are responsible for checking food and keeping the agreed plan.

When others do not understand

You do not need to debate or justify your child's needs every time. Keep the explanation calm and practical, share reliable information when useful, and set clear boundaries: adults must check with the parent or caregiver before offering food.

Birthdays and family celebrations

A note for grandparents, babysitters and other caregivers

You can print or share this page. Complete the blanks with the child's parent or legal caregiver before using it. Follow the family's current written plan.

Please read before offering food

This child has celiac disease. They need to avoid gluten, and food preparation and shared equipment may require special precautions to prevent cross-contact. Never assume a food is suitable based on its name or appearance. Do not give the child food, drinks, treats, vitamins or medication unless the parent or caregiver has confirmed it is appropriate. Check the current package and follow the family's instructions. If you are unsure, do not guess—contact the parent or caregiver.

This handout is a communication aid, not a complete care plan. The parent or legal caregiver must provide current, child-specific instructions and discuss them with each caregiver.

3. A child-friendly page: understanding my needs

This page is for a child to complete with a trusted adult. Children should never have to manage food-safety decisions alone.

What is celiac disease?

Celiac disease is a condition that means gluten can make your body sick. Gluten is found in wheat, barley and rye. Your grown-ups and healthcare team help make a plan so your food is right for you. You did not cause celiac disease, and it is not your fault.

You do not need to remember every food rule or check food by yourself. The adults caring for you are responsible for checking food and helping keep you safe.

People who help me

My feelings matter

Sometimes you might feel happy, frustrated, confused or left out. All your feelings are okay. You can talk to a parent, teacher or another trusted grown-up. You can ask questions as many times as you need.

Things I can say

School, friends and celebrations

You can enjoy games, sports, crafts, music and other activities with your friends. If snacks or food are part of an activity, ask your teacher or another grown-up to check with your parent or caregiver. You deserve to be included, and adults can help make a plan.

4. Communication card

ENGLISH

I have celiac disease. I need to avoid gluten and prevent cross-contact. Please check the ingredients in every part of the dish and explain how it is prepared, cooked and served. If you are unsure, please tell me honestly so I can make another plan.

FRANÇAIS

J’ai la maladie cœliaque. Je dois éviter le gluten et prévenir la contamination croisée. Veuillez vérifier les ingrédients de chaque partie du plat et m’expliquer comment il est préparé, cuit et servi. Si vous avez un doute, dites-le-moi franchement afin que je puisse choisir une autre option.

5. Family and friends dining guide

A person with celiac disease needs to avoid gluten and cross-contact. If you are unsure about a dish or preparation step, say so. We can make another plan together.

Important health note

This workbook is for general education and organization only. It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, a treatment plan or a food-safety certification. It cannot determine whether a food, product, kitchen, restaurant or meal is suitable for an individual with celiac disease. Food rules and product information can vary by country and change over time. Always check current information and consult your physician or a registered dietitian experienced in celiac disease for personal guidance.
